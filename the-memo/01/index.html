Section 01

Why we're building this

Private equity returns are driven by making the right decision as quickly as possible. In that order. We've spoken with more than 100 managers and the same problem keeps showing up: decision-makers have far more information than they can possibly use, and only two ways to handle it. Both bad.

Either you operate on instinct, with data sitting in PDFs, email threads, and portfolio systems that don't talk, and decision-makers running on gut. Or you hire large teams. Analyst to associate to VP to partner, signal degrading at every handover, errors creeping in. In a world of compressing asset management fees, this becomes a non-starter for mid-market PE firms. Neither actually solves the problem.

So we're building the AI Operating System for private equity firms and their portfolio companies. We embed agents across the GP and every portfolio company, connected directly to accounting, CRM, drives, and email. They pull data from PDFs, spreadsheets, and meeting notes to produce the outputs the firm uses to make decisions. The system knows not only how to build a leverage buyout model but that the VP should review it before it goes into the slides for the Managing Director and the investment committee.

The firms that learn to use the data they already have, fast and across the whole portfolio, will build an edge. Nothing about that view has shifted since we launched in February, if anything it's sharpened.

Section 02

Where we are today

We signed our first paying customer in April. Three signed LOIs in hand, approx $140K of contracted ARR if they all convert. Our pilot partners are running our product on real, commercially sensitive data, with about 8,000 documents processed to date. We got accepted into the 434 accelerator in Virginia. Obviously, we've also had our setbacks.

One real change in the market this month. The frontier labs went formally into the PE-services business. On May 4, Anthropic launched a $1.5B JV with Blackstone. Days later, OpenAI followed with a $4B DeployCo backed by TPG. "AI in our operations" went from a curiosity item to a fixed agenda item for every PE COO and CFO we talk to. Three months ago we were having to explain the category. Now the conversation starts with "Blackstone is doing this, what's our version?" Our job has shifted from evangelizing to being the ready answer when intent shows up.

Section 03

Traction

01
First paying customer. A specialist VC fund signed a one-year software license in April. Investor management system covering their cap table and LP distributions. We are working hard to make sure they become evangelists.
02
First LOIs signed. Three in hand, ~$140K cumulative. The anchor is a UK real-estate PE firm. We built an agentic lease and tenant ingestion tool, 8,000+ documents validated at 95%+ extraction accuracy, caught data-quality errors their team had missed for years.
03
Two pilots officially killed. The first was a Spanish developer we'd been talking to since January. We undersold how light-touch the engagement would be from their side; their perceived effort outweighed the urgency, and the pilot died at kickoff. The second was a PE-backed corporate in pharma. Weeks of deep engagement, in-person scoping, wireframe presented, term sheet verbally received well, then they went silent at the review stage. We're learning fast and locking the lessons in. Two rules came out of these two losses. Ship a working mock before the second meeting. Get LOIs signed inside two weeks of verbal commitment, not two months.
Section 04

Product

The use cases we took on early looked disconnected from the outside. Rent roll at one pilot partner, credit notes at another, IC memos at a third, bank statements at a fourth. To us, the end game has been clear from day one. Each of those was a known component of the larger system we set out to build. Paul Graham's advice on building things that don't scale first was the operating principle.

Thankfully, the thesis is paying off. The same underlying primitives (document ingestion, extraction, validation, human-in-the-loop review, write-back) run across every engagement. The product is becoming infrastructure, not bespoke builds.

Specifically this month:

01
A unified investor-management module in production with our paying customer (cap table, distributions, audit trail, manual overrides, NetSuite integration).
02
A tenancy-schedule agent for the UK partner. Ingests lease documents, classifies, extracts, queues for human approval before writing back to their property-management system. Same pipeline reused on the buy-side for acquisition DD.
03
An IC-memo engine for one of the LOI partners. 7-step agentic pipeline against a 121-field underwriting checklist. Running on real client data, demo this Thursday.
