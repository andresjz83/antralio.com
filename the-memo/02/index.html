Hi friends, supporters and partners.

A lot moved in the eight weeks since Issue 01. The most important piece is a focus decision we're in the middle of, so I'll start there.

Section 01

The focus question

We set out in February to build the AI operating system for private equity. The eight weeks since Issue 01 of this newsletter were uneven in an instructive way. Closing new contracts proved harder than we planned and the updates below show we paused more than we signed. Pilot partners pulled us deeper into their workflows and decided to follow some and not others. That is what this issue is about.

In June, I spent a week in Madrid meeting with PE professionals. Different funds, different roles, but basically the same thread in every conversation. We felt more urgency for solving one acute painful problem to a standard they can trust than for a fully fledged AI-native OS. Our pilot partners have been telling us the same thing with their wallets and their time. Every meaningful engagement has really been on the back of a highly-specific high-value problem. The full vision for the OS landed as tomorrow's conversation while the specific workflows landed as today's. Whether any one problem is pervasive enough to carry a product is part of what we're exploring.

So we're testing a narrower shape. Three candidate products instead of one platform story, each owning a complete workflow end to end (with integrations). The candidates have come out of the work below: investor management with a built-in audit trail for fund CFOs, document ingestion and reconciliation for asset owners (real-estate managers, family offices, infrastructure operators), and intuitive and auditable reporting for multi-family offices and funds-of-funds. All of them run on the same primitives (document ingestion, extraction, validation, human-in-the-loop sign-off), so four months of build carry over whichever way we end up heading.

Over the next few weeks we will be testing appetite for each one as a wedge strong enough to lead everything we do next. Pricing, urgency, repeatability. We'll report what we decide in the next issue.

Section 02

Updates from the wild

01
Paying customer going deeper. Our specialist VC fund customer helped us evolve the product in a new direction. The CFO doesn't want a system that trusts her accounting stack. She wants one that catches what even her accountants missed. We rebuilt around an audit view that reconciles subscription documents against their ERP and flags every discrepancy for human sign-off.
02
The lease product outgrew its first deal. The UK real-estate firm that signed the £50K LOI in May has stalled on their end. Thankfully, the bulk of what we built for that engagement kept moving. Other real-estate owners and managers are now evaluating it for proactive portfolio management and for acquisition due diligence, and the footprint has widened from shopping centers to office portfolios and from the UK to Spain.
03
Accuracy you can audit. A family office asked us to rebuild their quarterly investment reporting. Before automating anything, we reconciled line items to the cent against their existing workbook with 99% accuracy. For a buy-side team evaluating an acquisition, we took the same discipline further, an interactive lease-audit report where each of roughly 245 extracted figures links to the exact page of the source document it came from.
04
We walked away from one. In June we parked the work related to one of the three LOI engagements from Issue 01. The work was real but the ask drifted toward a bespoke financial model-building system where we felt we didn't have a particular edge and couldn't immediately productize. The decision was extremely hard but it freed capacity and focus.
Section 03

Team

Saraa, a CS undergrad who came to us through the Charlottesville accelerator, joined for the summer. In her third week she packaged our document-redaction tool and shipped it to the Microsoft Store, our first product in a public app store. The tool exists because our customers' documents carry personal and commercially sensitive data and GDPR requires it stripped out before anything gets shared outside the firm. She's now running point on the family-office reporting tool.

Jonah just moved to the Bay Area. The people building the frontier are concentrated there. Conversations about where the technology is heading happen there earlier and more often than anywhere else and hearing them early is worth a lot to us. Our product gets better every time the underlying models do, and being close to the source helps us build so that each capability jump lands as product improvement for our customers on day one. Capital and talent sitting next door doesn't hurt either.

Section 04

Four things we learned

01
Fund managers don't really care about the GP's P&L. They care about value creation at their portfolio companies because that's where fund returns (and the bulk of the team's financial interests) are really derived from. Software that makes the management company more efficient competes for a small software budget and needs sign-off from the very top of the firm, while deploying at a portco is a decision a single mid-level asset manager can make. PE is a much tougher ICP to sell to than to use as a distribution channel.
02
Sophisticated buyers in other domains can't spec AI. A six-person deal team told us in discovery, “we don't know what we need because we don't know what it can do.” Walking in with a menu of concrete likely use cases has beaten fully open-ended discovery.
03
Trust comes from showing errors. The audit view exists because a CFO told us she wouldn't trust output she can't check. Highlighting discrepancies for human review converted skepticism into adoption faster than any accuracy stat we quoted.
04
A $100K approval is a political event. A senior operator walked us through why a deal stalled on his side. Approving a $100K spend meant declaring a $2M problem to a wide circle of stakeholders, and that exposure, not the price, was the real blocker. The working lesson is to make the case safe for your champion to socialize, and to size the first commitment so it doesn't need the whole room.
Section 05

Capital

We still haven't opened a round. We want the focus test resolved first. A round raised on one sharp product beats a round raised on a broad story.

One more thing worth reporting. We've had approaches about acqui-hiring the team. Flattering, but we're not pursuing them. Four months in, with a paying customer, a product footprint spreading across asset classes and markets and the surface getting sharper, we think building is the better trade for everyone with a stake in Antralio, ourselves included.

As before, happy to chat privately if you're a likely participant when the round opens.
